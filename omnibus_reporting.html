The Omnibus Package: How the EU Rewrote Its Sustainability Agenda in One Year

In February 2025, the European Commission proposed the most significant rollback of EU sustainability regulation since the Green Deal began. By March 2026, it was law. Understanding it means looking across three frameworks at once.

Shivam AsthanaApril 2026

The EU's sustainability regulatory agenda was built on an interlocking set of frameworks. CSRD told companies what to disclose. CSDDD told them what to act on. The EU Taxonomy defined what counted as sustainable in the first place. Then came the Omnibus: the Commission's original proposal was published in February 2025, and after a year of negotiation the final Directive (EU) 2026/470 was published in the Official Journal on 26 February 2026, entering into force 19 March 2026, amending all three simultaneously. Framed as simplification, in practice it was a political recalibration of how far the EU was willing to push mandatory sustainability obligations in the near term.

Why it happened

By late 2024, the EU sustainability regulatory stack had become a flashpoint in a wider competitiveness debate. The Draghi report on EU competitiveness, published September 2024, flagged regulatory burden as a constraint on investment. Business lobbies, particularly manufacturing and mid-sized companies, argued CSRD's scope was disproportionate and CSDDD's due diligence obligations would create unmanageable liability exposure. The incoming Commission committed explicitly to simplification as a political priority.

What changed across each framework

01

CSRD

Before: 250 employees or €50M turnover, ~50,000 companies globally.

After: 1,000 employees and €450M turnover, ~5,000 companies, listed SMEs fully removed.

02

CSDDD

Before: 1,000 employees and €450M turnover, climate transition plan required.

After: 5,000 employees and €1.5B turnover, transition plan removed, application delayed to July 2029.

03

EU Taxonomy

Before: all activities assessed regardless of size, complex DNSH criteria applied uniformly.

After: 10% materiality threshold introduced, simplified templates, DNSH revised for usability.

The full legislative timeline

Feb 2025Omnibus published. Commission proposes simultaneous amendments to CSRD, CSDDD, EU Taxonomy.
Apr 2025Stop-the-Clock enters force. Wave 2 and 3 CSRD reporting delayed two years, CSDDD delayed one year.
Jul 2025Taxonomy delegated act adopted. EFRAG publishes revised ESRS exposure drafts, with a substantial reduction in datapoints relative to the original standards, the exact figure depends on which version is used as the baseline.
Dec 2025Provisional agreement reached. Parliament formally adopts 16 December. EFRAG submits final ESRS technical advice.
Feb 2026Omnibus I formally adopted. Directive (EU) 2026/470 published in the Official Journal 26 February. Council adoption 24 February.
Mar 2026Enters into force. 19 March 2026. CSDDD transposition due July 2028, application from July 2029.

The CSDDD changes deserve particular attention

Of the three frameworks, the changes to CSDDD were arguably the most significant in character, not just scope. The original required large companies to identify, assess, and address human rights and environmental harms across their entire value chain, with EU-level civil liability for failures.

Scope reduction: threshold jumped from 1,000 employees / €450M to 5,000 employees / €1.5B, roughly a 70% reduction in companies in scope.
Climate transition plans removed: the obligation to adopt a Paris-aligned transition plan was deleted entirely.
Civil liability softened: the EU-wide civil liability regime was removed. Member states retain the option under national law.
Penalties reduced: the ceiling fell from a minimum of 5% of global turnover to a cap of 3%.
Application delayed: pushed back to July 2029, two years later than the original timeline.

What the Omnibus did not change

CSRD still exists and still applies above the new thresholds. Double materiality is preserved. The ESRS structure survives, simplified. CSDDD's core risk-based due diligence framework also survives, what changed is scope, liability architecture, and timeline, not the underlying obligation. The EU Taxonomy's six environmental objectives and four alignment conditions, including Do No Significant Harm, remain unchanged.

Omnibus I narrowed the regulations. It did not end them. Capital markets have not pulled back from ESG expectations the way regulators have pulled back from ESG mandates.

The strategic reading

Companies that used the original timelines as a reason to build genuine data infrastructure, supplier engagement capability, and emissions accounting discipline are better positioned than those that treated compliance as a box to tick. That advantage does not disappear because the mandatory deadline moved. Investor pressure, lender requirements, and supply chain expectations from large buyers continue pushing in the same direction the regulation was pointing. The Omnibus changed the legal floor. It did not change the ceiling that market expectations create.
