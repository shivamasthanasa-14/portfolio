The EU Taxonomy: What Makes an Economic Activity Sustainable?

The EU Taxonomy is not a reporting standard or a disclosure framework. It is something more foundational: a shared, legally defined definition of what green actually means.

Shivam AsthanaApril 2026

When a company says an investment is green, or a bank claims its lending portfolio is Paris-aligned, those statements only carry weight if there is a common definition of what green means. Before the EU Taxonomy Regulation, established 2020 and entering force progressively from 2022, there was no such definition in European law. An activity is either taxonomy-aligned or it is not, with no ambiguity in principle, even where the application is technically demanding.

The six environmental objectives

An economic activity qualifies as sustainable if it contributes substantially to at least one of six objectives while doing no significant harm to the others. The first two went live in 2022, the remaining four in 2024. Taxonomy reporting covers the applicable environmental objectives and activities under the relevant delegated acts for a given company; the 2026 simplifications changed both the reporting population and the practical assessment burden.

What alignment actually requires

SCA

Substantial Contribution. The activity must meaningfully advance at least one environmental objective.

DNSH

Do No Significant Harm to the other five objectives. A wind farm damaging a protected marine ecosystem fails this test even while contributing to climate mitigation.

MSS

Minimum Social Safeguards. Operations must align with OECD guidelines and the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights.

Taxonomy alignment requires substantial contribution to at least one environmental objective, compliance with the relevant DNSH criteria for the others, and minimum safeguards. These three tests are applied, in practice, through Technical Screening Criteria, published sector by sector through delegated acts and regularly updated. TSC isn't a separate, independent fourth condition alongside the other three, it's the mechanism that operationalises substantial contribution and DNSH for each specific activity.

Eligible versus aligned

Taxonomy-eligible means an activity is listed as having the potential to contribute to an objective, it says nothing about whether it actually meets the criteria. Taxonomy-aligned means the activity is eligible, assessed against the technical screening criteria, confirmed to make a substantial contribution, and verified to do no significant harm. Companies report the share of revenue, capex, and opex that is eligible versus aligned, and the gap between the two figures is a meaningful signal.

What the 2025 and 2026 simplifications changed

Materiality threshold introduced: the 2026 simplifications introduced a materiality threshold under which certain economic activities below the relevant 10% KPI threshold may be excluded from detailed eligibility and alignment assessment, subject to the applicable denominator and aggregation rules, not a blanket exemption for every activity under that size.
Simplified reporting templates: shortened and restructured. Zero-value entries no longer need reporting.
DNSH criteria revised: particularly around pollution prevention, with a further systematic review committed by the Commission.

In March 2026, the Commission published draft amendments to the Climate and Environmental Delegated Acts proposing further revisions, with public feedback running through April 2026. This is an ongoing process, not a settled one.

A high gap between eligible and aligned revenue is a strategic signal, not just a compliance metric.

Why the Taxonomy matters beyond compliance

Investors and lenders use taxonomy KPIs to assess whether a business is genuinely positioned for the low-carbon transition or simply disclosing in the right format. For financial institutions, the Green Asset Ratio, calculated using counterparty Taxonomy data, is becoming a standard portfolio sustainability measure. CSRD tells companies what to disclose, the GHG Protocol tells them how to count emissions, the Taxonomy tells capital markets which activities are actually sustainable.
