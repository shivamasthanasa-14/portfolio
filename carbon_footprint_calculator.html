Where This Breaks Down, On Purpose

The composite company used across this track operates roughly 20 sites across 11 countries. Applying one Germany-specific factor to all of them, as the slider above lets you do, is not a real calculation, it is a demonstration of what happens when a single factor gets stretched further than it should. France's grid, dominated by nuclear power, runs at a small fraction of Germany's carbon intensity per kWh. Poland's, still coal-heavy, runs well above it. A real Scope 2 inventory needs a location-based factor per country at minimum, and ideally a market-based calculation using suppliers' own residual mix data where renewable energy certificates are in play.

The honest use of a tool like this: a fast, defensible order-of-magnitude estimate for a single-country operation, or a first-pass sanity check before building the real multi-factor version. Not a substitute for one.

Why The Factor Itself Moves

Even the "one number" isn't static

The Umweltbundesamt revises prior years as better generation and trade data comes in. The figure this calculator uses for 2025 could itself be revised downward next year, the same way 2024's estimate already was.

YearFirst published estimateLater revised figure
2023386 g CO₂/kWh379 g CO₂/kWh
2024363 g CO₂/kWh353 g CO₂/kWh
2025344 g CO₂/kWhnot yet revised

Worth building into any real reporting process: a note on which vintage of the factor was used, and a plan to re-check it before the number gets locked into an assured disclosure.

This calculator does one thing honestly: it shows exactly how consumption times a factor becomes a headline emissions number, and it makes the single biggest simplification, one factor for every site, impossible to miss. Sources and methodology note: Emission factor from the Umweltbundesamt's "CO₂-Emissionen pro Kilowattstunde Strom" series, 2025 figure (344 g CO₂/kWh), published covering 2025 generation data. This is the "Emissionsfaktor Strommix" figure, not the "Strominlandsverbrauch" variant that additionally accounts for Germany's electricity trade balance, which UBA reports as running a few grams per kWh higher. Household equivalent uses a 7 t CO₂e/year average European household footprint (EEA) for illustrative scale only. The composite company profile is the same one used throughout this track and does not represent a real employer. This tool intentionally applies one national factor across a multi-country profile to demonstrate the limitation, not to model a real inventory.
