The Construction Products Regulation Nobody's Talking About Yet

While CBAM gets the attention for taxing carbon at the border, the revised Construction Products Regulation quietly made carbon disclosure mandatory at the point of sale for concrete, steel, and insulation. It's the actual defining regulation for building materials, and it barely comes up in conversation.

Shivam AsthanaRegulation Series

The revised Construction Products Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2024/3110, entered into force on 7 January 2025 and began applying its priority-category framework from 8 January 2026. CPR is a staged regulation, GWP declaration becomes mandatory product by product as the relevant harmonised technical specification is updated, not as a single blanket switch flipped for every steel, concrete, insulation, and adhesive product on one date. But the direction is clear and moving fast: for products already covered by an updated standard, a Global Warming Potential figure is now part of the mandatory Declaration of Performance, not a voluntary add-on.

What's mandatory now

Rolling out since 8 January 2026, priority categories

CategoriesConcrete, steel, insulation, and adhesives are the named priority categories for the phased rollout, covered as their specific harmonised standards are updated, not all at once.
RequirementFor products already covered, GWP declaration, calculated per EN 15804, becomes part of the mandatory Declaration of Performance, the same document required for CE marking.
Scope pathApplies to products covered by updated harmonised standards as they're published, similar rolling logic to ESPR's delegated acts.

Where it's headed

Jan 2025Revised CPR enters into force.
Jan 2026Priority categories (concrete, steel, insulation, adhesives) begin rolling into mandatory GWP declaration, product by product, as each harmonised standard is updated.
2030Scope expands to 13 environmental indicators.
2032Full expansion to 19 indicators, effectively a complete lifecycle assessment requirement.

CBAM taxes carbon at the border. CPR asks for carbon on the product's own paperwork. Related emissions, calculated to different rules.

Why this runs alongside CBAM and ESPR, not instead of them

A steel producer selling into the EU is now managing three related but distinct obligations at once. CBAM attaches a cost to embedded carbon at import, calculated to its own methodology. CPR asks, for products already covered by an updated standard, for a GWP figure on the product's own Declaration of Performance, calculated per EN 15804. ESPR, once its iron and steel delegated act is adopted, an indicative 2026 timeline that has not yet happened, will likely add ecodesign and traceability requirements on top of both, under its own rules again. CBAM, CPR, and ESPR may concern related emissions, but their calculation boundaries, methodologies, and verification requirements are not necessarily identical, and a company still needs to check each one rather than assume one number satisfies all three.

Cross-reference: see the CBAM article and the ESPR article for how each of the other two treats the same underlying steel and cement emissions data.

What this means in practice

A building-materials manufacturer that builds a rigorous GWP calculation methodology once, and maintains clear documentation of exactly which boundary and standard it follows, is far better positioned to adapt that work for CBAM and, eventually, ESPR than a company starting each calculation from scratch. The three regulations don't share a number, but they draw on the same underlying emissions and production data, and that data infrastructure is the expensive part worth building once.
