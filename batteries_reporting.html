The Battery Passport Is the Test Case for Every Product Passport Coming After It

This regulation is relevant well beyond battery manufacturers. Any company whose product contains an industrial or EV battery inherits these obligations, and the Digital Battery Passport is quietly serving as the pilot for a concept ESPR will extend across many more product categories.

Shivam AsthanaRegulation Series

The EU Batteries Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, is easy to mentally file under "someone else's problem" if your company doesn't manufacture batteries. That's a mistake. The obligations attach to the battery itself, which means they follow it into whatever product it ends up inside, industrial equipment, EVs, energy storage systems, and beyond.

What's already active

Phased, several obligations already binding

18 Feb 2026Carbon footprint declaration requirements expand to rechargeable industrial batteries above 2 kWh, following the same requirement that already applied to EV batteries from February 2025. The exact scope varies by battery category, and these dates depend on the underlying delegated and implementing acts on methodology and format being published, if those acts slip, so does the date.
Aug 2026Labelling requirements take effect.
18 Feb 2027The Digital Battery Passport becomes mandatory for EV, light means of transport, and industrial batteries above 2 kWh, one of the first real-world tests of the Digital Product Passport concept.
Aug 2027Due diligence obligations begin, pushed back a year from the original 2026 date, applying mainly to operators above €40 million turnover.

Why the due diligence delay matters less than it sounds

The one-year push on due diligence wasn't a simplification in the Omnibus sense of narrowing scope. It was a coordination delay: the Commission is aligning battery due diligence guidance with the guidelines already being developed under CSDDD, so companies aren't running two separate due diligence programmes covering overlapping critical mineral supply chains, lithium, cobalt, nickel, and graphite among them.

Cross-reference: see the CSDDD article for the broader due diligence pass-through logic. Battery due diligence is a specific, aligned instance of the same underlying supply chain scrutiny.

Feb 2027Digital Battery Passport live.
Aug 2027Due diligence obligations begin, aligned with CSDDD guidance.

Nobody outside the battery industry is watching this regulation closely. Everyone building a Digital Product Passport for anything else should be.

The part worth actually paying attention to

The Digital Battery Passport is explicitly framed by the Commission as the pioneering implementation of the Digital Product Passport concept that ESPR will extend to far more product categories over the coming years. Whatever data architecture, digital identifiers, structured material declarations, lifecycle tracking, a company has to build to satisfy the Battery Passport by February 2027 is not a battery-specific investment. It's a rehearsal for whatever ESPR category comes for that company next.

Cross-reference: see the ESPR article for how the Digital Product Passport concept is expanding beyond batteries.

What this means in practice

Any manufacturer whose product contains an industrial or EV battery, not just battery producers themselves, should treat February 2027 as a real deadline for passport-ready product data, and treat the due diligence alignment with CSDDD as a reason to build one supply chain due diligence process rather than parallel ones for batteries and everything else.

Sources: Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 (EU Batteries Regulation), European Commission implementation timeline, and current industry analysis, current as of August 2026. Several of the staged dates above depend on delegated and implementing acts still being finalised, on methodology and declaration format specifically. Where those acts are delayed, the Regulation's own text pushes the associated date back accordingly.
